Why Idukki cardamom has a character of its own

How hills, shade, rain and careful hand harvesting shape the aroma of cardamom from the Western Ghats.

Cardamom is a plant of the forest understory. It grows best on cool, humid, shaded hillsides with steady rainfall, which describes much of the Western Ghats in Kerala. Idukki district sits in the middle of that landscape and is closely tied to the story of Indian cardamom. The surrounding region is often called the Cardamom Hills.

Hills, shade and rain

Elevation, regular rain and filtered light all influence how a cardamom plant grows and how its pods develop. Plantations in Idukki are commonly laid out beneath taller shade trees, which keeps the plants cool and protects the soil. Many are managed at a small scale, often by families who know their land closely.

A harvest that comes in rounds

Cardamom pods do not ripen all at once. A plant flowers and fruits over an extended period, so pickers return to the same plants several times in a season and choose pods at the right stage of maturity by hand. Picking at the right moment matters because it affects colour, aroma and how well a pod holds its character after drying.

Why origin matters to a buyer

For a buyer, origin is more than a place name. Knowing where a pod was grown, and who grew it, makes it easier to talk about consistency, to trace a lot, and to understand why one harvest smells and tastes the way it does. That is the idea behind Heritage Spices: staying close to the farmers of Idukki and keeping the conversation clear from the first sample to the final shipment.
