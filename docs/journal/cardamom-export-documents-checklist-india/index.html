Cardamom export documents from India: a buyer’s checklist

Quick answer: a cardamom shipment from India typically needs an invoice, packing list, bill of lading, certificate of origin and phytosanitary certificate.

Importers in the Gulf often ask which documents come with a cardamom shipment from India. The exact list depends on your country, your product and your clearing agent, so treat this as a starting checklist and confirm requirements locally.

Commercial documents

A commercial invoice states what is sold, the quantity, the value and the terms, such as FOB, CFR or CIF. A packing list describes how the goods are packed. For sea freight there is a bill of lading, and for air freight an airway bill.

Origin and plant health

A certificate of origin states that the goods come from India. A phytosanitary certificate, issued by the relevant Indian authority, confirms the plant product has been inspected for pests. Many countries ask for one for spices, so check your rules.

Exporter registrations

In India, exporters generally need an Importer-Exporter Code (IEC), and spice exporters are usually registered with the Spices Board. Ask any supplier for their registrations and check them. Heritage Spices does not yet claim these credentials on this site, and we will add them here once they are in place.

Quality and testing

Some buyers request a lab test report covering moisture and other quality or safety parameters. If you need one, say so at the start, because it affects timing and cost.

Tell us your country’s rules early

Import and labelling requirements differ between the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, Oman and Bahrain. Share yours with us first and your clearing agent can confirm the details. See how we work on our export process page, or read about importing cardamom from India.
