Why is cardamom so expensive? What drives the price

Quick answer: cardamom is labour-intensive to grow, pick and dry, and prices move with crop size, grade and demand.

Cardamom is often called the queen of spices, and it is one of the costliest spices by weight. The reasons are practical, and understanding them helps buyers read a quotation.

It is grown with a lot of hand work

Cardamom plants grow in cool, humid, shaded hill country such as Idukki in Kerala. The pods ripen at different times on the same plant, so they are picked by hand in several rounds across the season. That takes many hours of labour for a small quantity of finished spice.

Drying and sorting add effort

After picking, pods are cleaned, dried with care to keep their green colour and aroma, and then sorted by size and quality. Careful drying and sorting is a large part of what separates a bright, aromatic lot from an ordinary one.

Supply changes from year to year

Weather, rainfall and plant health affect how much cardamom a region produces. When the crop is smaller, prices tend to rise; when it is larger, they tend to ease. Demand from the Gulf, India and other markets also moves prices.

Grade and size change the price

Larger, greener, more uniform pods are generally priced higher than mixed or smaller lots. See our guides to cardamom sizes and Indian grades before comparing offers.

Quality and consistency cost more to deliver

Sorting for a uniform size, bright colour and clean, dry pods takes extra time. A lot that is consistent from the first bag to the last is easier for a buyer to use and resell, and that reliability is part of what buyers pay for.

Freight, packing and paperwork

The delivered cost also includes packing, transport, insurance and documents. These vary by destination and delivery terms, which is why two quotations can differ even for similar cardamom. Read our import checklist to compare offers fairly.
