How to import cardamom from India: a buyer’s checklist

A practical checklist for Gulf importers buying cardamom from India, from specification and samples to shipping terms and paperwork.

Importing cardamom from India is straightforward when both sides are clear from the start. This checklist covers the points that most often decide whether a first order goes smoothly. Rules differ by country and can change, so always confirm the latest requirements with your clearing agent.

1. Write your specification

Describe what you need: size in millimetres, colour, aroma, moisture expectations, packing size and the market you will sell into. Even a few lines give both sides something to check against.

2. Approve a sample first

Ask for a sample and compare it with your specification. Agree what the sample represents and what you will approve before anything ships.

3. Choose your delivery terms

Common terms are FOB, where the supplier loads the goods at the Indian port and you arrange the freight, CFR, where the supplier pays the freight to your destination port, and CIF, which adds insurance. Pick the one that suits how you already work with freight forwarders.

4. Check packing and labelling

Confirm packing sizes and what must appear on the label. Several Gulf markets have their own labelling expectations, including language requirements, so check with your agent before you finalise.

5. Prepare the documents

Shipments usually involve a commercial invoice, a packing list, a bill of lading or airway bill and a certificate of origin, plus any other certificates your destination requires. Ask your agent for the exact list for your port and product.

6. Plan for timing

Allow time for sampling, production, packing, booking and sea transit. If your business has seasonal peaks, such as Ramadan, plan the order well ahead.
