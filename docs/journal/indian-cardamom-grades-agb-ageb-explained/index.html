Indian cardamom grades explained: AGEB, AGB and AGS

Quick answer: Indian green cardamom is graded by pod size and colour, from extra bold (largest) to bold and superior. Millimetre ranges vary by trader.

If you buy Indian green cardamom you will meet grade names such as AGEB, AGB and AGS. They look confusing at first, but the idea is simple: larger, greener, more uniform pods are placed in higher grades.

What the letters stand for

AG usually stands for Alleppey Green, a trade name that goes back to the port through which Kerala cardamom was traditionally sold. EB means extra bold, B means bold and S means superior. So AGEB is Alleppey Green Extra Bold, AGB is Alleppey Green Bold and AGS is Alleppey Green Superior.

How the grades differ

In general, AGEB is the largest and most striking grade, AGB is a slightly smaller bold pod, and AGS and smaller grades follow. Each trader and market sets its own millimetre ranges, so never assume that one supplier’s AGB matches another’s. Ask for the size range in millimetres and a physical sample.

Bigger is not always better

Larger pods look impressive and are often chosen for retail packs and gifting. Coffee, chai and masala blenders may prefer a smaller grade with excellent aroma and good uniformity, which can suit their use just as well. Choose the grade for your use, not for the name.

What else to check besides grade

Grade only describes size and general appearance. Also check colour, aroma, moisture, the share of broken or empty pods and cleanliness. Our bulk buying checklist covers these, and this guide explains size more broadly.
