Cardamom in Arabic coffee: why the pod matters

Why green cardamom is at the heart of Gulf coffee, and what coffee blenders and importers should look for in a pod.

Across the Gulf, coffee is more than a drink. Serving Arabic coffee, often called qahwa or gahwa, is a gesture of welcome, and cardamom is what gives it much of its recognisable fragrance. Saudi, Emirati, Qatari, Kuwaiti, Omani and Bahraini versions differ in roast, colour and extra spices, but cardamom is a constant across them.

Why green cardamom

Arabic coffee is usually lightly roasted and brewed with ground or crushed cardamom, sometimes alongside saffron, cloves or other spices. Because the coffee is delicate, the cardamom is easy to taste. A pod that smells musty or flat will show up in the cup, while a fresh, sweet and clean pod lifts the whole drink.

What blenders and importers look for

People who blend or trade cardamom for coffee tend to focus on a few things. They look for a bright, even green colour, which usually points to careful picking and drying. They want a strong, sweet aroma when a pod is cracked open, and uniform size so that a blend behaves the same each time. Cleanliness matters too: whole pods with few splits, stalks or dust are easier to grind and pack.

Whole pods keep their aroma

Cardamom holds its fragrance best inside the pod. Once seeds are removed or ground, the aroma tends to fade faster, so many buyers prefer to buy whole green pods and process them close to the time of use. Whole pods also make it easier to check quality by eye and nose before you buy.
