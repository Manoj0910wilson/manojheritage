Cardamom harvest season in Kerala: when are pods picked?

Quick answer: in Kerala, cardamom is picked by hand in rounds over several months, mainly in the second half of the year.

Buyers often ask when the new crop arrives. In Idukki and the wider Kerala cardamom hills, harvesting is not a single day. Pods are picked by hand in rounds, roughly every few weeks, over many months, with the main picking falling in the later part of the year.

Why picking happens in rounds

Pods on one plant ripen at different times. Pickers return to the same plants again and again so that each pod is cut at the right stage. Picking too early or too late can affect colour, aroma and weight.

What this means for buyers

Because the crop comes in over months, availability and quality can vary through the season. If you need a particular size or a steady supply, talk to your supplier early and tell them the quantity and timing you need.

From plant to packed pod

After picking, pods are cleaned and dried carefully so they keep their green colour and aroma, then sorted by size and quality. The way a lot is dried and graded has a large effect on how it looks and smells when it reaches you. See our guide to what to look for in a premium pod.

Weather and the crop

Rainfall, temperature and plant health influence how much cardamom a region produces and when. A year with a smaller crop can mean tighter supply, while a good year can ease it. Buyers who plan ahead and share their needs early usually have more choice.

Fresh lots and older stock

Ask your supplier about the age of the lot you are buying, how it was dried and how it has been stored. Our guide to storing green cardamom explains how aroma is kept.
