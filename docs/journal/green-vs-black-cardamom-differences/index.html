Green vs black cardamom: the differences buyers should know

Green and black cardamom are different spices with different flavours and uses. Here is how to tell them apart before you order.

Many first-time buyers ask whether green and black cardamom are the same spice at different stages. They are not. They come from different plants and are used in different ways, so ordering the wrong one is a costly mistake.

Appearance

Green cardamom pods are small, light green and oval with thin papery skin, and are usually sold whole. Black cardamom pods are much larger, dark brown to black, wrinkled and rough.

Flavour and aroma

Green cardamom has a sweet, floral, citrus-like fragrance. Black cardamom is smoky, camphor-like and earthy, because the pods are traditionally dried over fire. The two are not interchangeable in a recipe.

Where each is used

Green cardamom is the spice in chai, Arabic coffee, desserts, sweets and rice dishes such as biryani and pulao. Black cardamom is used mainly in savoury, slow-cooked dishes and some spice blends.

Where they come from

Green cardamom (Elettaria cardamomum) is grown in warm, humid, shaded hills, and Idukki in Kerala is one of India’s best-known growing areas. Black cardamom (Amomum subulatum) is typically grown in cooler Himalayan regions.

What this means when ordering

Always state the spice, form (whole pods) and size or grade in your enquiry. If you are buying for Arabic coffee, chai or sweets, you need green cardamom. Heritage Spices supplies whole green cardamom from Idukki. See our green cardamom page, learn how sizes are described, or request a sample.
